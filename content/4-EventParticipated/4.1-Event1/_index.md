# Event Report: “Prove It: Agentic Cloud Investigation Series”

### Event Information

- **Organizers:** CloudThinker × First Cloud AI Journey (FCAJ)
- **Schedule:** September 5, 12, and 19, 2026 · 09:00–12:00
- **Location:** AWS Office, 26th Floor, Bitexco Financial Tower, 2 Hai Trieu Street, Saigon Ward, Ho Chi Minh City
- **Role:** Participant and member of **Team HCM15 (Tyrex)**
- **Format:** Three hands-on workshops using read-only AWS and Kubernetes demo environments

### Event Objectives

- Learn how to govern an AI Agent before granting it access to a cloud environment.
- Investigate cloud incidents by testing competing hypotheses and collecting traceable evidence.
- Practise working with AI Agents under explicit identities, permissions, guardrails, and operating boundaries.
- Build the ability to defend technical conclusions under time pressure.
- Produce three portfolio artifacts: Agentic Cloud Review, Incident Report, and Investigation Dossier.

### Program Principles

The program emphasized **evidence over confidence**. Participants did not provide personal AWS credentials and did not modify infrastructure. The demo environments were read-only: teams could inspect, analyze, investigate, and recommend, but could not execute remediation.

Each session followed the structure **LEARN → PRACTICE → SHARE**, combining guided concepts, hands-on investigation, and presentation of findings.

### Day 01 — GOVERN · Observe Before You Act

**Saturday, September 5, 2026**

#### Main Activities

- Examined how the security boundary changes when an AI Agent receives access to a cloud environment.
- Identified the Agent identity and designed permissions according to the principle of least privilege.
- Defined operating boundaries and guardrails before granting access.
- Used a read-only Agent to review an AWS demo account without changing its resources.
- Recorded observations, risks, and recommended follow-up actions based on evidence.

#### Outcome

- Understood why Agent governance must be designed before access is enabled.
- Distinguished read-only observation from permissions that can mutate resources.
- Practised supervising an Agent instead of automatically accepting its output.
- Completed the **Agentic Cloud Review** artifact and developed the **Agent Supervisor** skill.

**Reference:** [Day 01 — GOVERN](https://docs.cloudthinker.io/learn/workshops/prove-it/day-01-govern)

### Day 02 — INVESTIGATE · From Signal to Root Cause

**Saturday, September 12, 2026**

#### Main Activities

- Investigated a cloud environment that had been intentionally misconfigured.
- Challenged the Agent's initial answer instead of accepting the first explanation.
- Developed competing hypotheses and tested them against evidence collected from the environment.
- Recorded claims, sources, and supporting or contradicting evidence in an Evidence Table.
- Constructed a Causal Graph connecting the observed symptoms to the underlying cause.
- Consolidated the investigation into an Incident Report.

#### Outcome

- Improved the ability to distinguish observations, hypotheses, evidence, and conclusions.
- Practised rejecting unsupported claims and tracing each conclusion back to its source.
- Completed the **Incident Report** artifact and developed the **Agentic Incident Investigator** skill.
- Team HCM15 (Tyrex) achieved a score of **93.6/100** for the Day 02 submission.

**Reference:** [Day 02 — INVESTIGATE](https://docs.cloudthinker.io/learn/workshops/prove-it/day-02-investigate)

### Day 03 — OPERATE · Prove It Arena

**Saturday, September 19, 2026**

#### Main Activities

- Competed in the Prove It Arena as a member of Team HCM15 (Tyrex).
- Received an unfamiliar cloud environment and performed a rapid initial review.
- Built an Environment Map covering the main resources, dependencies, access boundaries, and potential risks.
- Responded to an incident inject under time pressure.
- Divided responsibilities for Agent operation, competing-hypothesis review, evidence collection, causal analysis, and dossier preparation.
- Collected evidence with identifiable sources and timestamps, constructed a Causal Graph, and prepared the Investigation Dossier.
- Defended the team's conclusions before the judging panel in the Final Defence.

#### Outcome

- Applied the complete investigation workflow: rapid review, hypothesis development, evidence collection, causal reasoning, decision, and defence.
- Completed the **Investigation Dossier** artifact and practised the **Agentic Cloud Operator** skill.
- The Day 03 result was below the team's expectations, revealing areas to improve in role assignment, time management, evidence verification, and technical defence.

**Reference:** [Day 03 — Prove It Arena](https://docs.cloudthinker.io/learn/workshops/prove-it/day-03-prove-it-arena)

### Arena Evaluation Rubric

- Agent governance and operating boundaries: **15 points**
- Environment review and risk prioritization: **20 points**
- Incident investigation and causal reasoning: **30 points**
- Decision and remediation quality: **20 points**
- Evidence defence and communication: **15 points**

Each hint incurred a two-point deduction, and claims without a traceable source received no credit. A conclusion of insufficient evidence could still receive credit when defended appropriately.

### Overall Series Results

- Completed all three stages: GOVERN, INVESTIGATE, and OPERATE.
- Produced the three portfolio artifacts: Agentic Cloud Review, Incident Report, and Investigation Dossier.
- Developed the Agent Supervisor, Agentic Incident Investigator, and Agentic Cloud Operator skills.
- Based on the team's combined performance across the three days, **Team HCM15 (Tyrex)** received the award **“Most Impressive Submission from Ho Chi Minh City.”**

### Key Takeaways

- An AI Agent must operate within a clearly defined identity, permission scope, and set of guardrails.
- The Agent's first answer is a hypothesis, not a verified conclusion.
- Every important claim should be traceable to evidence and its source.
- Competing hypotheses reduce confirmation bias during incident investigation.
- Effective teamwork requires explicit roles for Agent operation, evidence recording, opposing hypotheses, causal analysis, and report preparation.
- A technically reasonable conclusion is not enough unless the team can communicate and defend it clearly.

### Event Experience

The series provided a practical view of how AI Agents can support cloud operations without replacing human judgement. The most valuable lesson was to treat confidence as insufficient and require evidence before accepting a conclusion. Working with Team HCM15 (Tyrex) also highlighted the importance of role clarity, time management, and communication during an investigation.

Although the Day 03 performance did not meet the team's expectations, the experience exposed concrete weaknesses that can be improved in future incident-response exercises. Completing the full series helped connect cloud governance, investigation, and operations into one evidence-driven workflow.
